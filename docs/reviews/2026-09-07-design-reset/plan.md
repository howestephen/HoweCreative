## The position the site should establish

**Creative technologist with a creative lead's judgement and the ability to make the work.** The supporting proposition is: Stephen turns complex technology into clear interfaces, compelling media and reusable creative workflows, connecting art direction with hands-on implementation.

That connection is the value. It means considering a brief's story, visual language, technical constraints and delivery together. It is substantiated across projects: UNCX brand and product work, its reusable 3D/motion system, Academy's learning experiences, Quiver's complete generative production, Badger's implemented product and Solana Diary's API-connected media with human approval. Twenty years of mixed practice does not mean equal seniority in every specialism, and the site should not imply that it does.

**Primary audience:** creative technology and creative AI hiring managers. **Strong adjacent audience:** product companies combining interface, brand and motion work. **Selective audience:** specialist design engineering and product design roles, supported by the relevant implementation and decision evidence. This conclusion draws on six employer descriptions, with location and retrieval limits in the [market report](../2026-09-07-market-reset-positioning.md) and [original target-role report](../2026-09-07-role-evidence-research.md).

Granola places greater weight on frontend depth. These are different hiring needs beneath overlapping titles. Do not turn the homepage into a job-title selector. Establish a coherent practice, then let an employer inspect relevant proof.

**Showcase cards:** use a project-specific icon, project name, short concrete explanation, role/status and an obvious open action. The cards do not use full project cover graphics. Make a deliberate spatial arrangement that settles into a readable collection; the desktop study uses three columns and two rows. Mobile should allow a readable vertical collection rather than shrinking six cards into one viewport. Discipline remains the primary filter and software the secondary filter. Projects can belong to several disciplines, and filter states must be recoverable. Software and role labels require project evidence rather than inferred skill ratings.

**Full archive:** provide a direct route from global navigation and the showcase. The complete archive retains all eligible work and earlier collections, with discipline/software filters and clear empty-state recovery. Selecting six projects for the opening does not reduce the agreed archive-review scope. The older [HTML board](index.html) still previews the earlier complete image-card index; it has not been updated to demonstrate this sequence.

**Open case study:** the selected card advances towards the viewer and opens a large, stable reading panel. The glass diffuses the scene behind it while text, media and controls within the panel stay sharp. Other cards recede and lose focus; foreground/background particles establish depth around the reading surface. Use sufficient surface opacity for sustained reading. Show the actual images, films, process, contribution credits, status and verified interactive demos here. Keep a visible close control, restore focus and collection position on close, and support shareable project URLs and browser Back. A directly opened project must be readable without first playing the hero. Inside a case, scrolling moves through the case content rather than the background scene.

**Audio:** default off with explicit activation and a persistent mute control. Use the same transition state for picture and sound: dispersion can open a quiet texture, and bounded scroll speed can affect its agitation or brightness. Smooth parameter changes and let the sound settle as movement stops. Fade interface audio while project media plays and suspend it when the page is hidden. Audio carries atmosphere, not instructions or essential information.

**Depth and accessibility proof:** prototype actual 3D particle positions and camera movement alongside projected, readable interface surfaces. Demonstrate a coherent relationship between card depth, focus and glass diffusion before committing to a renderer strategy. A screen-wide blur alone does not prove depth. Keep case-study content as selectable, accessible HTML; the background is inert while the reading panel is open. Test keyboard focus, Escape/Back, touch scrolling, reduced motion, rendering failure and a readable mobile presentation. Three.js documents [depth-of-field post-processing](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/BokehPass.html); CSS [backdrop filtering](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/backdrop-filter) blurs pixels behind a translucent surface. These are separate mechanisms. The prototype uses custom particle softness and CSS glass; a full-scene depth-of-field pass has not been implemented or tested. Follow the [WAI modal-dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/) for the reading panel's focus and keyboard behaviour.

### B. Type as architecture

**Premise:** the portfolio index becomes the sculptural identity. Work is the structure of the site.

**First screen:** a tightly composed wall of large project names in ivory, black and one cobalt accent. The names have shallow physical depth and strong typographic hierarchy. Stephen's role stays clear in a fixed margin. Current work dominates; an explicit earlier-work section exposes all six collections. The index remains readable as ordinary text.

**Signature interaction:** focus or hover brings a title forward, allowing light and a tightly framed project preview to enter its letterforms. Selection carries the camera through a typographic aperture into a readable case study. A touch selection previews; its explicit project link opens the case. There is no compulsory exploration or orbiting camera.

**Meaning and structure:** the same typographic construction binds very different media into one practice. All current work is present at the outset. On phones the titles form a vertical composition, with an active depth treatment instead of a miniature desktop scene.

**Feasibility and risk:** extruded title geometry, clipped media and a single camera move are known mechanisms. Art direction depends on exceptional typography, careful preview cropping and preserving legibility. Long titles and multiple video decoders are the principal constraints. Use one active preview and keep equivalent HTML links. Strongest for immediate portfolio discovery; less expressive of tool-building than A.

### C. The cutaway

**Premise:** visitors can inspect the construction of finished creative work.

**First screen:** a cinematic triptych representing three different outcomes: a finished film, a designed interface and a 3D production system. Each has generous space and a short, precise contribution credit. A visible control invites the visitor to reveal the making.

**Signature interaction:** moving that control separates each outcome through depth into selected source, design decisions and finished result. Quiver might expose planned endpoints and authored typography; Badger could show an implemented flow and its states; the motion system could expose a reusable scene. Each uses its real construction, rather than an invented universal workflow. A layer can be pinned for a short explanation or opened as a full case study.

**Meaning and structure:** authorship and technical judgement become visible beneath the finish. The cutaway behaviour continues inside project pages, while a complete conventional index stays available. The CV remains a direct route.

**Feasibility and risk:** layered meshes and controlled camera movement are feasible, but valuable source artifacts must first be selected for three projects. Avoid presenting a conceptual layer diagram as the actual engineering architecture. This is strongest for explaining existing work; it risks becoming dense and repeating the media-heavy opening the user already rejected.
