# Portfolio market reset: practitioner evidence

Research date: 7 September 2026. These are primary website texts and creator-authored making-of accounts, not live browser observations or screenshot assessments. Interaction behaviour below is source-described. Professional implications and limitations are my assessment. Current roles and publication establish relevant practice; none establishes that a website caused a hire or commission.

## Six useful benchmarks

**1. Rauno Freiberg: a catalogue of interaction judgement.** The [Craft archive](https://rauno.me/craft) names individual behaviours, including precision sliders, toolbars and a video scrubber, and distinguishes prototypes, production links and essays. His [interaction essay](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design) explains interruptibility and includes a radial-menu exercise. The organising idea is inspectable craft. Professionally, this supplies evidence of decisions beneath polished motion. Visitors can move from a small experiment to reasoning or production work. Limitation: many small specimens leave visitors to infer the scale and business purpose of larger engagements. His [homepage](https://rauno.me/) identifies work with Vercel and Devouring Details.

**2. Emil Kowalski: useful products become the demonstration.** His [site](https://emilkowal.ski/) connects named tools with explanatory writing and identifies his role at Linear. In [Building a toast component](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component), visitors are invited to add notifications, compare interruption behaviour and try gestures. Interaction makes engineering judgement testable. The route to evidence includes the implementation rationale, documentation and product itself. Limitation: this is narrow, component-oriented proof; its quiet index would undersell Stephen's film, brand and 3D range. Borrow the inspectable behaviour, not the visual shell.

**3. Matt DesLauriers: a practice connected by process.** His [portfolio](https://www.mattdesl.com/) links generative art, installations, software and research through code and generative processes. [The Sferic Project](https://www.mattdesl.com/sferics) connects field recording, audible examples, photographs and a documented technical contribution. The organising idea is a traceable investigation across media. Listening has evidential value because the sound is source material. The route to context is a readable project essay. Limitation: the homepage explicitly calls itself a placeholder, so this is a model for connecting disciplines, not a recommendation for its presentation.

**4. Stefan Vitasović: assembly becomes a motion language.** His [making-of](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/05/case-study-stefan-vitasovic-portfolio-2025/) describes characters assembling into words, offset typography, and consistent video treatment across projects. Scroll moves from the introduction to the project overview; the [site](https://stefanvitasovic.dev/) also exposes Projects and About links. The motif demonstrates coordinated typography, media and rendering craft, relevant to his stated lead creative developer role at 14islands. Cost: custom scrolling and media rendering increase complexity. The creator explicitly removes WebGL on mobile. A distinctive desktop experience still needs a deliberate mobile composition.

**5. Jonas Reymondin: one navigation instrument, several contexts.** The [creator's account](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/03/16/jonas-reymondins-portfolio-reclaiming-the-ui-eye-through-systems-code-and-pixel-motion/) describes a strict grid and a bottom navigation component that changes between page links, project controls and section navigation. Its keyboard controls and complete project list are useful functions. The professional claim is the combination of design precision and engineering systems. Home, Work and About provide a readable structure around expressive motion. Limitation: contextual controls must remain understandable as their state changes. He reports improved visibility, without publishing hiring conversion evidence. The [live site](https://jonasreymondin.com/) timed out in text retrieval.

**6. Artem Shcherban: information occupies a second layer.** His [making-of](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/02/designing-against-the-gallery-a-two-year-journey-to-a-layered-portfolio-experience/) describes project visuals accompanied by compact information panels, expandable mobile details and a downloadable resume. Layering allows art direction and project meaning to coexist. This demonstrates hierarchy and content design more than rendering virtuosity. The [site](https://artemshcherban.com/) exposes Portfolio and Resume routes. Cost: hidden information needs strong discovery cues, and his process consumed two years and six concepts. The transferable principle is consistent access to facts beside expressive work; the homepage carousel adds little for Stephen.

## Two original directions to develop

**A responsive identity.** Create an explicitly labelled personal site study: one full-viewport composition with oversized typography across the upper left, an original sculptural letterform on the right, and a live product fragment cut into the lower edge. One large control moves between three authored moods. Type width, material, motion timing and optional sound change together. The visitor is testing a coherent identity across brand, product, 3D and audio. A small “How this responds” link reveals the decisions and workflow; Selected Work stays visible. This earns its interaction through deliberate cause and effect. Cost: it needs excellent original art direction and a strong default still.

**A film you can interrogate.** Start with one beautifully composed moving-image sequence, with a narrow vertical rail of visible chapter marks. Selecting a mark freezes the shot and opens an asymmetric cutaway: the final frame remains large while its actual storyboard, model or generated source, sound stem and workflow decision appear alongside it. Credits identify precisely what Stephen made. “Continue film” closes the cutaway; “Read project” leads to full evidence. This turns cinematic authorship into inspectable production knowledge, including software and API work where relevant. Cost: source assets must support the story, and loading and sound require careful handling.
